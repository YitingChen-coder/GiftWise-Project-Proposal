01 / The people behind the idea

Three conversations.
One connected problem.

Buying for friends, partners, or family should feel thoughtful. Finding the right gift—and knowing what to pay—can feel like three separate jobs.

From three real potential users interviewed by me. Excerpts below are translated from the Chinese interview records I provided; they are not original English statements.

01

Where should I buy it?

One product.
Too many prices.

“Sometimes I don’t know where to buy it, or which platform offers the best price.”

Design implicationCompare equivalent products across retailers, with a clear source for each verified price.

02

What should I buy?

A budget isn’t
a gift idea.

“Sometimes I’ve already set a budget for a gift, but I still don’t know what to buy.”

Design implicationStart with budget, recipient, and interests; refine recommendations through conversation.

03

Is this a good price?

A discount needs
some context.

“I hope I can directly check a product’s historical prices … That way, I can plan my budget earlier.”

Design implicationShow observed price history, with coverage gaps and uncertainty made explicit.

These conversations shaped three pillars: gift discovery, price comparison, and historical price context. Three qualitative interviews inform the direction; they do not establish representative market demand.

02 / Going deeper

The focus is AI.
The standard is trust.

I want to learn how to turn natural-language gift needs into useful, grounded recommendations—not just fluent suggestions. The planned app will call models itself, retrieve product offers through tools, and evaluate whether its answers respect the shopper’s constraints.

Structured preferences and free text will work together. Shoppers can change their minds through multi-turn refinement without starting over.

HOW I’LL KNOW IT WORKS

Useful is measurable.

  • Recommendations stay within the stated budget.
  • Gifts reflect the recipient’s interests and preferences.
  • Compared offers match the same product variant.
  • Displayed prices have a retrieved source and timestamp.
  • Evaluation tracks verified-price coverage and unsupported claims.

Planned evaluation criteria, not measured results.

03 / From proposal to project fair

An ambitious idea.
A deliberate first version.

Start with the riskiest dependency: access to real product and historical price data. A few reliable journeys are more valuable than unsupported promises of universal coverage.

NOV10

Kickoff / De-risk the data

Prove the sources first.

Validate product-search and historical-price providers, terms, cost, free-tier limits, and coverage. Define product matching and price provenance; design the schema and prototype the recommendation-to-price journey.

FOUNDATION
NOV17

v1 / Working end to end

A small, trustworthy shopping journey.

Deploy guest-first AI gift recommendations from structured or natural-language input, verified offer comparisons from validated integrations, and real third-party historical prices for supported products. Show date coverage and unavailable states.

Data dependency: if history access fails, disclose the gap and narrow product coverage. Never generate substitute history.

V1 DELIVERY
NOV24

Thanksgiving / No class

Room to refine.

Continue testing and refinement as appropriate; this is not an official delivery milestone.

NO CLASS
DEC01

v2 / Project fair

More context. More continuity.

Add AI purchase guidance grounded in observed history, optional Clerk sign-in, saved favorites, gift lists and search history. Strengthen evaluations and polish the experience; broaden retailer coverage only where feasible.

PROJECT FAIR
Not in the promise

Universal product or retailer coverage, certain predictions of future discounts, checkout or payments, unauthorized large-scale scraping, native mobile apps, and production price alerts.

The evidence comes before the promise.

Amazon, Walmart, Best Buy, and Target are desired sources, not confirmed integrations. Provider access, licensing, costs, product coverage, and data quality remain to be verified. Match model, size, color, condition, and pack quantity; show source, time, currency, availability where supported, and whether shipping or taxes are excluded. Clearly separate observed prices from uncertain future estimates.

Use free tiers; set available usage limits and spending alerts. Verify costs before selecting providers. No future application services are configured for this proposal.
