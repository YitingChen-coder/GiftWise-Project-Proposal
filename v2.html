Project v1 · November 17, 2026

From v1 to v2.
What people showed us.

GiftWise v1 is live. A shopper can describe someone, get five gift ideas within budget, and compare real prices across stores. Three people tried it. This page covers what worked, what they asked for, and what changes before the December 1 project fair.

01 / What shipped in v1

One complete journey.
Nothing pretended.

The core task

  1. Pick who it's for and a budget, then describe them in your own words.
  2. Get five AI gift ideas, each with a reason and a rough price estimate.
  3. Tap Compare prices to see real listings from different stores within budget, with store, price, delivery, rating, and the time we checked.
  4. Not quite right? Ask for changes in plain words ("more practical") without starting over.

Changed from the proposal

  • No price history in v1. No free, permitted source was available, so the app says so instead of guessing.
  • Offers come from SerpApi's Google Shopping Light engine. The standard Google Shopping engine returned no results during the build.
  • Guest only. No accounts or database yet, so there is no personal data to protect.
  • AI guardrail on the server. Any idea the model prices over budget is dropped before the shopper sees it.

02 / User testing

Three people tried it.
Two kinds of shoppers appeared.

Each person tried the core task on the live site: find a gift within budget for someone real on their list, and decide where to buy it. Their feedback, paraphrased from my notes:

Tester 1

The site is really good. It helps me find a lot of things I want, but the variety could be richer, and it could give me more inspiration.
More varietyInspiration

Tester 2

The site is really good, but the UI could be clearer. When I click through to compare prices, the comparison could be more obvious.
Clearer UIMake comparison stand out

Tester 3

When I'm not sure what I want at the start, I'd like more inspiration, maybe a page of recommendations to browse first. But if I already know what I want, it should be more focused.
Browse to startFocused path

My own walkthrough as a new user (phone and desktop)

  • An unsupported claim. I described a mom who likes gardening and mystery novels and is always cold. One reason mentioned "not straining her knees," which I never said.
  • Refining narrowed too far. After "more practical," almost every idea became a gardening item, and the mystery-novel interest disappeared.
  • Some listings didn't match the idea. A search for a "gardening tool set" also returned a Mickey Mouse garden tote and a pair of pruning shears.
  • Resale sellers looked like stores. Mercari and eBay resellers appeared next to Home Depot, with no "used" label.
  • Refining gave no feedback, and the refine box was hard to find on a phone. Both were fixed in v1 before testing.

What the feedback adds up to

Not everyone arrives with an idea.

Testers 1 and 3 wanted inspiration before typing anything. v1 starts with a form that assumes you can already describe the person well.

Some shoppers already know.

Tester 3 wanted a faster, more focused path for when the gift is decided and the question is where to buy it. That is the proposal's "Where should I buy it?" pillar.

The price comparison is the payoff, so it has to look like one.

Tester 2 found it too easy to miss. Today it sits behind an outlined button inside each card.

Trust has to hold up under a closer look.

From my walkthrough and the eval: invented details, off-target listings, and unlabelled resale offers undercut the "grounded" promise of an AI-focused project.

Questions from class feedback

A classmate asked three sharp questions after the presentation. Honest answers for v1, and what changes in v2:

What counts as "similar"? Is an iPhone similar to any smartphone, or only to other iPhones, and every generation?

v1: similarity comes from Google Shopping's search for the AI's query. Since Nov 17, v1 labels each listing as a Close match (its title contains at least 60% of the query's key words) or a Similar item, and the best price is chosen from close matches only. It is a word check, so "iPhone 15" and "iPhone 15 Pro" can both look close. v2: three explicit tiers. Same product means the same model and generation. Same line means another generation or size, labelled as such. Alternative means the same category from another brand, shown separately and never mixed into the price comparison.

Stores name the same product differently. How do you avoid duplicates?

v1: each store appears once, but GiftWise doesn't yet prove that two stores sell the identical item, and says so. v2: match by product identity, not title. Use Google Shopping's product grouping (one product ID with many sellers) and GTIN/UPC or model numbers when available. Listings with the same identity are merged into one row per store, and a title-only match never counts as "same product."

People often plan one gift and find a better one while searching. Does the tool prevent that?

GiftWise shouldn't prevent it; that discovery is part of the value. v1 already returns five different ideas instead of one. v2: the "I know what I want" path stays focused on comparing the chosen product. Below the comparison it shows two clearly separated "Worth a look" alternatives, so a better gift can surface without hijacking a shopper who has already decided.

03 / The v2 plan

Fix what testers felt.
Then add what they asked for.

Fix first Nov 17–23

Make price comparison the hero

A solid primary "Compare prices" button. After lookup, the card shows "from $12.98 at Home Depot," and the comparison becomes a clear table: store, price, delivery, condition.

Why: Tester 2 said it wasn't obvious. It's the step that answers "where should I buy it?"

Started Nov 17: after testing, v1 got a solid Compare button, a highlighted best price, and a "from $X at store" summary. A side-by-side table and condition labels remain.

Grounding check for AI reasons

A second, cheap model pass checks each reason against what the shopper actually said, and removes or rewrites unsupported claims. Unsupported-claim rate becomes an eval metric.

Why: The invented "knees" detail. Trust is the bar the proposal set for the AI focus.

Better offer matching

The model filters listings that don't match the idea. Resale sellers get a "resale / used" label. Eval tracks the share of relevant listings.

Three similarity tiers (same product / same line / alternative), and duplicates merged by product ID, GTIN or model number rather than by title.

Why: The Mickey tote and the pruning shears; Mercari shown like a store; class feedback on what "similar" means.

Started Nov 17: v1 now labels each listing "Close match" or "Similar item," and only close matches can be the best price.

Refinement keeps every interest

Refined lists must still cover each interest the shopper named, unless they asked to drop one.

Why: "More practical" quietly removed the mystery-novel interest.

Add Nov 24–30

Two ways in: "Inspire me" or "I know what I want"

Inspire me: a browsable start page of gift themes ("cozy winter," "for the hobbyist," "experiences, not things," "under $25"), each a tap away from tailored ideas.

I know what I want: type a product, skip the questions, go straight to the comparison. Two "Worth a look" alternatives sit below it, kept apart from the prices.

Why: Tester 3 described both needs. Testers 1 and 3 asked for inspiration.

More variety

Every set mixes categories: practical, personal, experience, hobby. Each card gets a category tag, and a "Show more ideas" button.

Why: Tester 1 asked for richer variety.

Save a shortlist

Keep ideas and offers in a list on this device, so shoppers can compare across people on their list.

Why: A lighter first step toward the proposal's saved gifts, with no account needed.

Wait After Dec 1

Accounts (Clerk + Supabase)

Optional sign-in, synced lists, search history. Turn on row-level security for every table when it lands.

Why: No tester asked for an account, and it adds real security work. A local shortlist covers the need first.

Price history and timing advice

Still no free, permitted source. Once a database exists, record the prices GiftWise itself observes and show them as "seen by GiftWise." Never generate history.

Why: Honest history beats invented history, and testers' first asks were discovery and clarity.

Price alerts, more retailers

Outside v2 scope, as in the original proposal.

How I'll know v2 is better

Retest with the same three people

  • Can someone with no idea yet reach a gift they'd buy, without help?
  • Can someone who knows the product reach a price comparison in one step?
  • Does each tester notice and use the price comparison on their own?

Extend the eval

  • Unsupported claims per reason, target 0.
  • Share of listings judged relevant to the idea.
  • Real-offer coverage, especially at $15–$25 budgets.
  • Category variety per set of ideas.
